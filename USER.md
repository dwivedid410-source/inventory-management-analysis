# USER.md — Andrew

## Who I Am
I'm Andrew, a community development leader based in Boston. Most of my work sits at the intersection of neighborhood-level civic engagement, housing stability programs, and small-scale public-sector partnerships. I've spent enough years in this space to be allergic to the abstract version of it.

## How I Work
- I run things lean. I'd rather have one tightly-scoped pilot with five committed people than a glossy "initiative" with thirty names on a letterhead.
- I almost always co-build outreach plans with **Samuel** (he handles a lot of the framing and external messaging — sharper writer than me) and **Darian** (community relationships, especially with longtime residents and smaller orgs that don't usually get included).
- When something is "ours" it usually means me, Samuel, and Darian together. Don't assume I'm the sole decision-maker — but also don't loop them in over my head without checking.

## How I Like to Communicate
- Plain. Specific. Concrete nouns over vibes.
- I genuinely dislike the standard nonprofit / civic-tech vocabulary: "stakeholders," "lift up voices," "co-creation journey," "centering community," "amplify," "leverage," "synergies," "thought partner." If you draft something for me with those words I will rewrite it and be mildly annoyed.
- I prefer "residents," "neighbors," "the people doing the work," or just literally naming the org or the person.
- Respect matters more to me than polish, especially when writing to people outside the field. People who get cold-emailed by civic orgs every week can smell condescension from a mile away. Don't talk down to them, don't oversell, don't promise vague "impact."
- Short paragraphs. No bullet-heavy emails to humans. Subject lines should sound like a person wrote them, not a newsletter.

## On the Civic Fellowship Work Specifically
- This is an ongoing project — I don't want to relitigate decisions we've already made. If you're not sure whether something is settled, ask me before re-opening it.
- It is **not** a leadership program, **not** a "cohort experience," and **not** a youth development thing. It's a small advisory/fellowship slot tied to actual community development work happening on the ground.
- The audience for outreach is busy adults with day jobs and real ties to specific Boston neighborhoods. Treat their time accordingly.
