# User Trajectory — Public Comment Response Template for Zoning Variances

**Persona:** Andrew, Deputy Director of Community Development, City of Boston, MA.
Manages zoning and land use policy in a hybrid arrangement. Department
regularly receives public comments on zoning variance requests. Darian
(cross-team coordinator) flagged legal-risk language in current ad-hoc
responses. Department meeting next week.

Format: 12 turns, first-person user voice only. Short and precise (1–3
sentences each). Single-purpose per turn. Natural progression with small
evolving decisions. No assistant responses.

---

## Turn 1
hey im andrew, deputy director of community development for boston. my team currently winds it on responses to public comments on zoning variance requests and darian flagged some of the language as a legal risk. i need to put together a word doc template with different response scenarios — but first i want to see what other cities are actually doing, not just make it up.

## Turn 2
pull examples from 4-5 comparable cities — somerville, cambridge, philly, minneapolis, seattle are good candidates. only use real published documents you can link to, dont paraphrase from memory.

## Turn 3
skip the somerville one if its just a one-page FAQ — i want actual response templates or guidance documents, not citizen-facing summaries.

## Turn 4
based on what you found, list the distinct comment scenarios these cities are templating for. i want to see the categories before we pick which ones to include in our template.

## Turn 5
lets use 6 scenarios: support, opposition on traffic/parking grounds, opposition on density/character grounds, procedural objections, requests for more info, and out-of-scope comments. drop anything else for now.

## Turn 6
before drafting — list the specific phrases or commitments that create legal exposure (per darian's concern). things like promising outcomes, agreeing with characterizations, etc. ill use that as a do-not-say list inside the template.

## Turn 7
now draft the word doc. structure: 1-page intro with usage guidance and the do-not-say list, then one section per scenario with (a) when to use it, (b) the response template with [bracketed] fill-in fields, (c) 1-2 sample sentences. neutral municipal tone — not warm, not cold.

## Turn 8
the opposition-on-density template reads too defensive. rerun it through the humanizer to sound respectful and acknowledging without conceding anything substantive. keep it under 150 words.

## Turn 9
add a footer to every template that says "This response does not constitute a determination on the variance application. The Zoning Board of Appeal makes the final decision per [cite]." check the actual boston citation for that and fill it in.

## Turn 10
save as `zoning_comment_response_template_v1.docx`. add a version/date header at the top and a "Prepared by: Andrew, Deputy Director of Community Development, City of Boston" line so the provenance travels with the file.

## Turn 11
draft a gmail to darian.m@boston.gov, subject "Draft: Zoning Variance Comment Response Template — feedback before dept meeting." short body: thanks for flagging the issue, attached is v1 based on practices from [cities used], please focus review on the do-not-say list and the density-opposition template, need feedback by end of week.

## Turn 12
set a reminder for thursday 4pm to follow up with darian if i havent heard back, and save to memory: template v1 exists, awaiting darian review, presenting at dept meeting next week. thats it for now.
