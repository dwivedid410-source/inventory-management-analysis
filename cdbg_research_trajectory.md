# User Trajectory — CDBG Academic Research for Policy Brief

**Persona:** Andrew, Deputy Director of Community Development, City of Boston.
Masters degree, comfortable with academic writing (Tier 2 interest). Writing a
policy brief on CDBG fund allocation for his department. Needs 3–4 recent
papers to cite. Limited time — not doing a full literature review.

Format: 14 turns, first-person user voice only. No assistant replies.

---

## Turn 1
quick thing. im writing a policy brief about how we allocate CDBG funds in our department and i want to cite some actual research to back up a few points. specifically looking for recent papers on whether CDBG spending actually moves the needle on neighborhood outcomes like housing quality, employment, that kind of thing. can you find me like 3-4 solid papers from the last 5 years? i need the citations and a quick summary of what each one found

## Turn 2
oh actually before you start — peer reviewed is preferred but i dont need to be a purist about it. urban institute, brookings, HUD pd&r working papers are all fine, my director treats those as credible. just flag clearly whats peer reviewed vs whats a think tank report so i can tier them in the brief. and "last 5 years" — lets say 2020 or newer, but if theres a really seminal piece from like 2018 that everyone else is citing, mention it

## Turn 3
hm ok of those four the first two look great but the third one is really about LIHTC not CDBG, those are different programs even though they get confused. can you swap that out. and the fourth is from a journal i havent heard of, can you tell me if its actually a real refereed journal or one of those pay-to-publish things? not trying to gatekeep but i cant put a predatory journal citation in front of the director

## Turn 4
ok that journal is legit, ill keep it. one thing i want to narrow on — housing quality outcomes specifically. i have a whole section in the brief about housing rehab dollars and whether they actually improve unit conditions vs just maintaining the status quo. do any of these speak to that directly? if not, can we find one more thats really squarely on housing rehab effectiveness. employment effects are interesting but secondary for me

## Turn 5
the spader & rieger paper is exactly what i needed for the rehab section, great. now zoom out — im realizing all four are quantitative. for balance can we add one that takes more of a qualitative or case study approach? a paper that interviews program staff or grantees, or does a comparative case study across cities. policy briefs land better when theres a mix of "the numbers say x" and "and heres what implementation actually looks like"

## Turn 6
on the case study one — there was a paper a couple years ago about CDBG-DR (disaster recovery) administration that i think was out of texas a&m or somewhere? or maybe it was just CDBG generally not the DR variant. comparing administrative capacity across small and large grantees. ring any bells? if you can find it id rather use that than something generic because it actually maps to a point im making about local capacity

## Turn 7
ok that wasnt the one i was thinking of but its actually better, use it. citation format — city uses chicago author-date for policy work, not APA. can you redo all the citations in chicago 17 author-date style. and for the in-text format i need both the parenthetical (Author Year) version AND a full reference list entry for each one

## Turn 8
the summaries are good but theyre too long for what i need. can you cut each one down to: (1) what they did in one sentence, (2) the headline finding in one sentence, (3) one specific number or effect size if the paper has one — and thats it. like 3 sentences max. ill expand in the brief myself if i need to, i just need the gist plus the killer stat

## Turn 9
hold on — the theodos & gonzalez paper actually found that CDBG had pretty modest effects on neighborhood income, which kind of cuts against the argument im building in section 2. i dont want to just hide that. how do other recent papers position that finding? is it the consensus or an outlier? i need to know if im about to write something thats going to get pushed back on by anyone who actually knows the literature

## Turn 10
ok so its a real tension in the literature, not a fringe finding. thats fine actually, i can work with that — ill acknowledge it in the brief and pivot to "thats why targeted allocation matters" which is sort of my thesis anyway. while were here, anything in these papers about equity outcomes specifically? like differential effects across race or income tiers within neighborhoods. boston cares a lot about that lens and the director will absolutely ask

## Turn 11
perfect. one more ask — for each paper can you pull 1 direct quote, short, that i could drop into the brief as a pull quote. with the exact page number so i can verify. i wont use all of them but i want options. nothing more than like 25 words each, anything longer gets unwieldy in a brief

## Turn 12
ok lets get this into a file. save everything as `cdbg_sources.md` — structure it as: section per paper, with header = short title, then a metadata block (authors, year, journal/publisher, peer-reviewed yes/no, DOI or url), then the 3-sentence summary, then the pull quote with page number, then the full chicago reference at the bottom of the section. and at the very top put a one-paragraph "synthesis" that summarizes what the five papers collectively say, so future-me can skim it in 30 seconds

## Turn 13
two of these are paywalled right? can you check which ones have open access versions — preprints on SSRN, author copies on personal pages, or the HUD pd&r ones which i think are always free. add an "open access link" line to the metadata block where applicable. if its paywalled and theres no legit free version just note "paywalled, BPL access via library card" because i can pull it through the boston public library system

## Turn 14
last thing before i go write — am i missing anything obvious? like is there a major recent paper or a well-known author in this space that i should at least be aware of even if im not citing them, so i dont look uninformed if the director or someone in the meeting brings them up? give me like 2-3 names or papers as "good to know, not citing." then im done, i need to actually go write this thing
